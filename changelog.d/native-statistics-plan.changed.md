The native backend evaluates and finishes its statistics natively from a plan lowered once (SEP 47 section 6.4): the half-maximum widths, the species reduction, the multi-channel electron density and peak ionisation rate, the plasma integration ratios, the profile statistics and a general modal basis's flux statistics (`omega0`, `physical_energy`, `spectral_flux`, `analytic_flux`, `fwhm_t`, `fwhm_t_outer`) no longer return to Python, and a general modal plane's statistics no longer copy the field to the host. Their z-dependent inputs are read from the problem's own tables. A profile statistic (`density`, `pressure`, `core_radius`) reads the propagation's table of its path where one reproduces the profile, or the profile tabulated once over the run; its measured error, at most 1e-13 of the profile's magnitude (about 1e-15 on a gas-filled capillary), is recorded in the run's execution record under `statistics_tables`. A modal power weight that varies in z is summed in a different order, a rounding-level change. The four on-axis plasma statistics (`on_axis_electron_density`, `on_axis_peak_ionisation_rate` and their species forms) scan the axis row natively, agreeing with the host scan to the node scans' parity tolerance (1e-10 relative), and a spatial run's `beam_radius` and `fwhm_r` are finished natively, so the default statistics of a radial or free-space run no longer leave native code. `zdw` is read from a table of each mode's zero-dispersion wavelength along z made once before the run (exact for a mode that does not vary along z, otherwise within 1e-6 of the wavelength, with the measured error under `statistics_tables`), and the native backend records `nan` where the band holds no zero, as `numpy_ref` does, instead of raising. Every periodic-capable statistic is a native periodic row. The native backend refuses by name the metric statistics of a general modal basis whose metric varies inside one of its intervals. On every backend, a statistic the problem does not carry is refused by name before the run starts instead of at the first saved plane: a modal statistic such as `omega0` on a spatial run, a spatial statistic such as `beam_radius` or `absorbed_power` on a modal run (where `numpy_ref` used to return the peak ionisation rate as `absorbed_power`), the plasma integration diagnostics without a field-grid plasma column, and the electron density or peak ionisation rate without a plasma response.
